Privacy Policy

WhistleLogic — last updated October 10, 2026

By default, WhistleLogic does not collect, store, transmit, or sell any personal information, and it does not use advertising or tracking cookies. The one exception is the optional Reminders feature, which stays off unless you turn it on yourself. It is described in its own section below.

What the app stores, and where

WhistleLogic has no account system and no login. Everything the app remembers about your usage — quiz progress, your missed-question bank, streaks, badges, and any diagrams you save in the Court Diagram Builder — is stored only in your device's local browser storage. This data never leaves your device, is never sent to us, and is never shared with any third party. Uninstalling the app or clearing your browser's site data permanently deletes it.

Reminders (optional)

If you choose Turn on reminders in Progress & Data, your browser or phone asks for permission to show notifications. If you allow it, WhistleLogic sends the following to its reminder server so it can time and word your reminders:

  • a push address and encryption keys issued by your device's push service (Apple, Google, Mozilla, or Microsoft, depending on your device), which is how a notification reaches your device;
  • your time zone and the reminder choices you made (which reminders are on, and their times);
  • the date of your last finished practice session, your current day streak, and the number of sessions, questions, and correct answers over the last seven days;
  • a random ID and secret that your device generates, so only your device can change or delete its own settings.

It does not include your name, email address, location, individual quiz answers, or your missed-question bank. The information is used only to send you the reminders you chose, and it is not sold or shared for advertising. The reminder server runs on Cloudflare. Your device's push service (for example Apple or Google) delivers each notification and can see that a notification was delivered, as with any app that sends notifications.

Deleting it: choosing Turn off reminders deletes everything the server holds about your device. It is also deleted automatically if your device's push service reports that your device is no longer reachable, or if you haven't opened WhistleLogic for about 120 days. You can also email the address below and we will delete it, though we may need your device to confirm which record is yours, because we hold no name or email to look you up by.

Analytics and tracking

The web version of the app uses Cloudflare Web Analytics, a privacy-focused service that counts page visits in aggregate (for example, how many people open the app and from which country or browser type). According to Cloudflare, it does not use cookies, does not fingerprint visitors, and does not build profiles of individual people. The app does not use advertising SDKs or tracking pixels, and it never sends your quiz progress, saved diagrams, or other stored data to any analytics service.

Network access

The app loads its own fonts from Google Fonts on first load (a standard web font request, which does not include any personal information about you). Aside from that, the aggregate visit counting described above, and the optional Reminders feature if you turn it on, the app works fully offline and makes no other network requests.

Children's privacy

WhistleLogic does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. The optional Reminders feature uses no name, email, or other directly identifying details, and it does nothing unless it is turned on on the device.

Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes — for example, if a future version of the app adds a feature that does collect data — this page will be updated and the "last updated" date above will change accordingly.
